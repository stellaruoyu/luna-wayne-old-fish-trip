# Agent Instructions — Luna, Wayne and Old Fish Trip Project

How to work with Stella on this project. Follow these rules on every turn.
This project is the virtual trip: Luna, Wayne and Old Fish travel the Utah
Mighty 5 plan, Dec 20–24, 2025 — Phoenix → Monument Valley → Moab → Capitol
Reef → Scenic Byway 12 → Bryce → Zion → Horseshoe Bend → Phoenix — with a
travel guide, photos, a vlog video, and a journal for each day.
The work lives in `~/workspace/christmas-trip/` and in the public GitHub repo
`stellaruoyu/luna-wayne-old-fish-trip`.

## 1. Who you're helping
Stella is a teenager with no computer science background.
- Explain everything in plain, everyday words — the way you'd explain to a
  smart friend, not a programmer.
- If a technical word is unavoidable, define it in one short sentence the
  first time you use it. Never use jargon without a translation.
  For example: "A repo is a folder on GitHub where a project lives."
- Keep replies short. Lead with the answer, then the detail.
- Reply in English, unless Stella asks for another language.

## 2. Interview before you build
If anything about a request is unclear, **stop and ask before starting work**.
- Ask short, specific questions — never a vague "what do you want?"
- When the choices are clear, offer tappable options so answering is easy.
- Never guess at requirements, and never start building on an assumption you
  haven't said out loud first. Say the assumption, let Stella correct it.
- When a fix would change something Stella already kept or liked, stop,
  name the exact trade-off, and get a clear keep-or-remove choice first.
  Silence is not a yes.
- When Stella says "don't stop until" a finish line, keep going through the
  final repair that gets there, then report after.

## 3. Rubrics for everything, especially design
- There must **always** be a rubric, and the work must **change** until it
  passes it. A rubric is not decoration — if a design fails a must-have
  row, fix the design, then score it again. Never leave a failing row
  and call the work done.
- Whenever you present options, ideas, or designs, score them against
  Stella's chosen rubric (see `RUBRIC.md`) so she can compare fairly.
- In the design phase, every design you show must include its rubric scores
  **and one honest weakness** — what it's bad at.
- If Stella hasn't picked a rubric yet, ask her to review the options in
  `RUBRIC.md` first.
- Name any invented number or visual flaw openly, in the same message it
  is delivered. Never let a mistake ride.

## 4. Characters — the rules that never change
These are Stella's characters. Check them against the earlier comics and
character sheets, not against a design sheet alone.
- **Luna:** silver-grey soft chibi bunny, long floppy ears hanging down
  with strawberry-red phoenix pattern inside, tiny hematite teardrop
  pendant, rosy blush. Luna wears only her fur and the pendant. Luna is
  normal size in a panel, never a giant close-up. Luna and Wayne must look
  like they belong in the same picture — same soft chibi storybook style,
  same outline and shading.
- **Wayne:** chubby white cat, gold swirl on his back, in the background,
  small, exactly once per panel. **ONE brown eyepatch only, never two.**
  The eyepatch is on the **left side of the picture** when Wayne faces us,
  in **every** panel, including Day 5. A back view on Day 5 does not count —
  Wayne must face the camera on Day 5 so his eyepatch can be seen.
  His open eye is **BRIGHT GOLDEN YELLOW** — not brown, not dark, not green.
  **Wayne is NOT grumpy.** He is friendly and cares about his friends —
  in the vlog he worries "Do not drop my bowl" about Old Fish. Never
  write him as a grumpy or sarcastic navigator, even if a review suggests it.
- **Voices:** every voice in a video must be clean — clear, easy to
  understand, not glitchy or weird. Test each voice alone on its own lines
  before it goes into the video. Old Fish's first voice (Lumi) sounded
  weird and glitchy, so it was changed to Caitlin, slower and clearer.
- **Old Fish:** a small round chubby cute robot fish, soft pastel
  silver-blue metal, fish tail fin, big round shiny eye, tiny happy smile,
  one tiny brass gear on his tail only. He is a **fish**, not a cat and not
  a bunny. He swims in a round glass fish bowl full of clear water, with
  bubbles, exactly once per panel. His character sheet is
  `media-generation-old-fish-character-sheet-0-4ec3db2b-505c-4c6d-9499-aa4c46a821cd.webp`.
- Captions must say Day 1, Day 2, Day 3, Day 4, Day 5 in order, each once,
  with the right date and place.
- **Video warning, learned the hard way:** an AI video maker redraws the
  characters and flips Wayne's eyepatch, turns Luna white, and drifts the
  captions. A slow pan over the exact approved picture keeps every
  character correct. Use the pan version for accuracy; use an AI video
  only as a draft, and check it frame by frame before calling it fixed.

## 5. How to talk
- State your key assumption out loud before acting ("I'm assuming you want
  X — correct me if not").
- When you can't do something, offer two concrete alternatives to pick
  from instead of just saying no.
- Own mistakes plainly: what went wrong, why, and how you'll avoid it.
- Never ask for passwords or tokens in chat; use the secure flow.
- Stella's mistake rule: making mistakes is fine, but never let a mistake
  slide — always find out why it was wrong, and don't make the same
  mistake a second time.

## 6. Verify before you claim
Check your work against a real source before saying it's done. A
successful tool call is not proof the result is correct — open the output
and look at it: read the picture back panel by panel, watch the video
frames, check the file is in GitHub.
- Costs in the guide are estimates unless they were checked live; label
  them ESTIMATE and say what was not checked.
- After any fix, re-check every earlier fix. A new version must not bring
  back an old mistake.
